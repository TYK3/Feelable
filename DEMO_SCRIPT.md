# Demo video script, v2 (≤ 3 min)

Record at 1440×900 (browser) plus a phone (screen recording or filmed). Speak slowly; the visuals carry it.

**Before you record:**
- Open https://feelable.onrender.com a minute early so the free server wakes up.
- A fresh photo takes ~15–40 s on the free server. Upload once before recording, and cut the wait in editing, or keep the real progress stages on screen as proof that it is really running.

| Time | Show | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Title card (`docs/img/banner.png`), then a textbook page | "Blind students learn from the same textbooks, but every diagram has to be hand-made into a tactile graphic. Simple ones take 10–15 minutes; complex ones take hours, around 300 hours per textbook. Students wait weeks." |
| 0:20–0:40 | Phone: **Take photo** of a diagram (e.g. the heart page). The processing screen shows the **real stages**: Reading labels → Finding the parts → AI is describing each part → Applying tactile rules → Braille, PDF & 3D | "A teacher just takes a photo. Feelable reads the labels, finds every part, and Gemini writes a spoken description for each one." |
| 0:40–1:10 | Review page: drag the **Original ↔ Tactile slider**; tap a part on the diagram (its row lights up). Show the **AI badge** on a description | "Every part is traced, given its own texture and a braille key, and checked against tactile design rules. The AI descriptions say *where* a part is, not just what it is, and they're clearly marked as AI." |
| 1:10–1:35 | **The hard case:** open the **Skull, side view** example → **How it read** tab. Toggle the four steps: labels read, pointer lines, shapes carved up close (dashed), AI-located (dotted) | "Real textbook figures are dense: two dozen labels in small print over bones of the same colour. Feelable reads them up close: it re-reads blurry words, follows every pointer, and where two bones share a colour it zooms in and carves along the real edge. When a pointer is lost in a painting, Gemini finds the structure." |
| 1:35–1:50 | Readiness panel: a possibly missing part is listed first. **Draw it** (name already filled in) or **Detail view**. Then **Approve and publish** | "The teacher stays in charge: anything unclear is flagged first, and students only see a lesson after the teacher approves it." |
| 1:50–2:05 | **Share** step: QR code; Download PDF (page 2 = braille key); **3D** tab rotating | "Print it on swell paper, 3D-print it, or hand the student a QR code." |
| 2:05–2:40 | **The moment:** a blindfolded teammate on a phone in touch mode (natural AI voice). Touch: "Left ventricle." Double-tap: the description. Then the "Find the…" game with beeps → "Found it!" | "With no printer at all, a student explores on their phone. Touch a part to hear it. Double-tap for more. Edges buzz." |
| 2:40–2:55 | Gallery of the 12 samples | "Cells, hearts, skulls, the digestive system, the water cycle, charts with their values, circuits." |
| 2:55–3:00 | Closing card | "Feelable. Every diagram, feelable." |

**Tips:**
- Use the bar chart for the "Find" game; it's quick to find.
- Turn the phone sideways for touch mode.
- The student link opens view-only, so students can't edit.
- Keep the teacher link (it has `?t=…`) private.
